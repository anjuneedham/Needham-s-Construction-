# Organic Video Content Plan — Needham's Construction

TikTok first, Instagram Reels second, Facebook third. One shoot, cut three
ways. No paid spend, no fabricated numbers — this is a shooting and posting
habit, not a campaign.

---

## 1. Why TikTok is the lead platform

- Construction/trade content performs unusually well organically on TikTok —
  process footage (tiling, pouring concrete, framing a roof) is genuinely
  satisfying to watch, and the algorithm doesn't require an existing
  following to get real reach the way Facebook's does.
- It's also the cheapest format to produce: a phone held steady, vertical,
  15–60 seconds. Everything you'd shoot for TikTok reposts almost unchanged
  to Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels.
- Direction of repurposing: **TikTok → Instagram Reels → Facebook Reels/feed
  → website.** Never shoot three different things for three platforms.

Your account already has at least one video live
(`https://vt.tiktok.com/ZSq4neW3m/`, now linked from the home page's "See Our
Work" row — see §7). Once you share what's actually in it, its caption/title
on the site can say something specific instead of the generic placeholder
that's there now.

## 2. Content pillars (match the four real services)

Every post should map to one of these. Don't invent a fifth category —
they're the same four services the website and social bios already use.

| Pillar | What it looks like | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tiling** | Setting out, cutting, laying, grouting; finished bathroom/kitchen/pool-surround walkthroughs | Visually satisfying process shots; strong before/after |
| **Plumbing** | Pipe runs before a wall closes, fixture install, a fix mid-repair | Builds trust — people can *see* it's done properly, not hidden behind drywall |
| **Masonry** | Block-laying, formwork going up, a pour, the strip-down reveal | The "oddly satisfying" clip category — this already works for you (the steps and column-capital photos are exactly this kind of content, just needs the video version) |
| **General Construction** | Framing, a roof build story, a turret/addition going up | Biggest "wow" factor — the finished standing-seam roof you just added to the site is a full before→after arc: framing → sheathing → finished |

A fifth, cross-cutting pillar worth running alongside these:

| **Q&A / trust** | 15–30s answering one real question on camera ("How long does a bathroom re-tile take?", "Do you handle the whole job or just tiling?") | Cheapest content to produce — no site visit needed, just a phone and an answer |

## 3. Format by platform

### TikTok (primary)
- **Vertical (9:16), 15–45 seconds.** Longer only for a genuine build story with several stages.
- **Hook in the first 1–2 seconds** — show the result first, or state the job ("Redoing this bathroom shower — here's how it went"), not a slow intro.
- **On-screen captions/text**, always. Most people watch muted.
- **One clear subject per video.** Don't cram tiling + plumbing + masonry into one clip — that's three posts.
- Trending audio helps reach, but don't force it — a lot of trade content performs fine on voiceover or just ambient job-site sound (drill, trowel, water running).
- Post consistently over chasing virality. 3–4 solid posts a week beats one "perfect" post a month.

### Instagram
- **Reels** = same file as TikTok, same crop, re-uploaded (not shared as a link — native uploads perform better).
- **Feed carousels** for before/after and finished-job photos where a still image tells the story better than motion (e.g. a tiled floor, a finished facade). This is where the site's existing photo galleries (masonry steps, roof-finished, the pool projects) become a 3–5 slide carousel with almost no extra work.
- **Stories** for same-day "on site today" behind-the-scenes — lower effort, keeps the account looking active between Reels.

### Facebook
- **Repost the same Reel** (Facebook Reels, not just a feed video — Reels get more organic reach than a plain video post now).
- Facebook's audience for a contractor skews toward an older, more local, more "asking in a community group" crowd than TikTok/Instagram. Worth occasionally posting the **longer version** of a story (a 60–90s full walkthrough) as a native feed video, and cross-posting to relevant local Facebook groups (Jamaica home renovation / buy-and-sell / parish community groups) when the job is finished and the client's fine with it.
- Facebook Business Page **Services** section should list the four services with the same wording as the website (already covered in `SOCIAL-PROFILE-SETUP.md` §3–4).

## 4. A realistic posting cadence

This assumes one person (you, as ops lead) filming alongside actual jobs —
not a dedicated content person. Built to survive a busy week.

| Platform | Frequency | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok | 3–4x / week | 1 clip per active job site, cut on the spot |
| Instagram Reels | Same clips as TikTok, same day or next day | Re-upload, ~2 min |
| Instagram feed/carousel | 1–2x / week | From finished-job photos you're already taking for the website |
| Facebook Reels | Same clips as TikTok | Re-upload, ~2 min |
| Facebook feed (longer form) | 1x / week or per finished job | Longer cut of a build story |

**The habit that makes this sustainable:** film for 30–60 seconds at three
points on every job — start, midpoint, finish. That's your TikTok clip,
your Instagram carousel source, and your website project photos, from a
single filming habit, not three separate efforts.

## 6. Caption formulas

Keep the same voice as the rest of the brand (see `SOCIAL-PROFILE-SETUP.md`
§5 — plain, specific, no hype, no unverifiable claims). Three formulas that
work for trade content without sounding like an ad:

1. **The job, plainly stated.**
   > Re-tiling a main bathroom shower — marble-look porcelain, frameless
   > glass enclosure. [Location if the client's OK with it.]

2. **The reveal / before-after.**
   > This started as a bare block wall two weeks ago. [1–2 lines on what
   > changed.] Swipe for the before.

3. **The direct answer.**
   > "How long does this take?" Depends on size, but a standard bathroom
   > shower re-tile is usually [X] days once materials are on site.

**Hashtags:** a handful of specific ones beat a wall of generic ones —
`#JamaicaConstruction #KingstonContractor #TilingJamaica` style, matched to
the actual service and parish, not `#construction #build #home #reels` spam.

**Never:** invented stats ("20+ years", "500 jobs completed"), a "before"
photo that isn't genuinely from before the job started, or a client's
property shown without their OK.

## 8. Why the TikTok video isn't embedded as a player

The site deliberately never loads third-party embed scripts (TikTok's,
Instagram's, or Facebook's) — every page stays fast because nothing outside
your own files has to load for the page to render. TikTok/Instagram/Facebook
videos show as a card with a play badge that **opens the real post** when
clicked, rather than an inline player. That's a one-line change in
`src/data/*` for any future post — same pattern the one you sent uses now.

If you'd rather see actual TikTok video content playing inline on the site
long-term, the honest options are: (a) download the video from TikTok
yourself and add it as a self-hosted file the same way the two bathroom
walkthroughs are handled, or (b) accept the link-out card. Both are already
supported; neither requires new code.

## 9. Simple tracking (no vanity metrics)

Check monthly, not daily — obsessing over daily view counts on a new account
is a fast way to quit. Track only what tells you if it's working:

- **Saves/shares** on TikTok and Instagram (a stronger signal than views for
  whether content is actually useful to someone)
- **DMs/comments asking about a quote** — the real business outcome
- Which **pillar** (tiling / plumbing / masonry / general construction /
  Q&A) is getting the most engagement, so you shoot more of what's working

Nothing here should turn into a claim on the website ("as seen on TikTok",
follower counts, etc.) unless it's real and you want it there — the site's
own rule against invented numbers applies to social proof too.
